Can AI safely assess patients’ needs before they step into a doctor’s office? A study led by researchers at Google and [Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center](https://bidmc.org/) (BIDMC) and published in The Lancet today, offers an encouraging first look. As Google’s first-ever publication in the main journal of The Lancet, this research marks a significant milestone.

In a real-world clinical study at BIDMC’s ambulatory primary care clinic, 98 patients consulted [AMIE](http://goo.gle/amie), our research diagnostic AI chatbot, ahead of urgent care visits. Supervising physicians monitored the interactions in real time, and not a single conversation needed to be interrupted based on predefined safety criteria. And clinicians reported that the AI summaries helped them prepare for visits in 75% of cases and influenced their approach to care in more than half. AMIE’s differential diagnoses also matched the doctors’ final diagnoses 90% of the time.

While larger clinical trials are needed to assess patient-facing AI at scale, the findings suggest the potential for AI to enhance the patient-physician relationship and ease the strain on healthcare workers. And they demonstrate meaningful progress as we push the boundaries of what’s possible for [AI in science and health](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/ai-applications-science-people/).
