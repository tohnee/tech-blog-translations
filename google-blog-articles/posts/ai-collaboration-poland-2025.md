During a keynote at our Research@ event in Poland last week, Yossi Matias, Head of Google Research shared how his team’s work is guided by the "[magic cycle](https://research.google/blog/accelerating-the-magic-cycle-of-research-breakthroughs-and-real-world-applications/)" — where real-world challenges drive foundational research and scientific breakthroughs.

Our latest [Research@](https://www.youtube.com/watch?time_continue=1&v=3ZRpjMniPus&embeds_referring_euri=https%3A%2F%2Fresearch.google%2F&embeds_referring_origin=https%3A%2F%2Fresearch.google&source_ve_path=MzY4NDIsMjg2NjY) event gathered hundreds of researchers, academics, policymakers and partners to explore how we continue this cycle, turning complex research into real-world solutions that serve people in their daily lives.

A clear theme emerged across every conversation: collaboration. From deploying [Google Earth AI](https://ai.google/earth-ai/) to help tackle [public health](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZxmB8Z5i1Ls) and [disaster response](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8-macH8ozr4) challenges, [creating new methods for brain mapping](https://blog.google/technology/research/liconn-connectomics/) and supporting responsible [AI literacy](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/education/ai-quests/) and [education](https://research.google/blog/learn-your-way-reimagining-textbooks-with-generative-ai/), to developing AI that can act [as a virtual collaborator](https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/#:~:text=Explaining%20mechanisms%20of%20antimicrobial%20resistance) for scientists — partnership has been key, and we’re excited to see what we can build together next.
